STOP 10 — MOLEN DE HOOP / FORTIFICATION WALLS

[VOICE — calm, with space]
In front of you is Mill De Hoop.

The city council had this stone flour mill built in 1736.

A mill only works when there is wind. And that later became a problem.

In the nineteenth century, the trees on the canals grew so high that they obstructed the wind. Therefore, the mill hull was increased by approximately 5 meters 60.

That explains the strikingly slim shape you see now.

Then look around you.

You are standing on part of the old fortress.

These ramparts once meant something completely different. Here were moats, defenses, gates and soldiers. The boundary between inside and outside was literally visible here.

Now it's green.

You walk there.

Maybe there's a dog walking.

Maybe someone is sitting on a bench.

But the shape of the landscape still betrays the old fortress.

[PAUSE — 2 sec]

That is special about Tholen.

You no longer have to see the walls to understand that they were once here.

[TRANSITION — walking instruction]
We are now going to the Grote Kerk.

Walk back from the mill in the direction of the Oudelandsepoort. So you walk back over the rampart towards the place you just came from.

Along the way, pay close attention to the house named 'Molenvliet'.

Walk past this house and enter the walking path that is directly next to Molenvliet.

Follow this path for about 70 meters until Regentessestraat.

The name of this street recalls the orphanage that stood here from 1631 to 1786. It was an institution for orphans and was governed by regents and regents. The orphanage was part of the social life of the city: children without parents received shelter and care here.

When you walk here, you can no longer see anything of the orphanage itself. But the street name keeps the memory of this special place in the history of Tholen alive.

From the Regentessestraat we walk further to the Grote Kerk.
