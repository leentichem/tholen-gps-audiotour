STOP 04 — CITY HALL

[VOICE — quiet, slightly more solemn]
In front of you is the old town hall of Tholen.

But before you look at the facade, you have to go back to one day: May 16, 1452.

On that day a huge city fire broke out. About five-sixths of the houses were destroyed. The guest house, the town hall and several gates were also lost.

Imagine the panic.

A medieval city with many wooden parts, roofs close together and hardly any means to stop a major fire.

And yet a new town hall was built.

The building was built here around 1460 and is still in front of you in a modified form. The facade had eight footrests and canopies. The images that were once imagined there ultimately did not materialize.

Also look at the stone platform. This was made in 1758 by Nicolaas Muts from Middelburg.

This is where the daily life of the city was governed, judged and decided upon. Criminals were also tried in front of the town hall. The last death sentence was carried out here by sword in 1750.

And then there is the carillon.

There are 37 bells in the tower. One of the oldest carillon bells in the Netherlands dates from 1458. Nine other bells date from 1627.

[PAUSE — 2 sec]

If you're lucky, you'll hear the fifteen minutes soon.

This building actually tells one big story: a city can be hit hard and still start over.
