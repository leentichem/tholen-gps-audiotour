STOP 06 — STOOFSTRAAT

[VOICE — visual]
The name Stoofstraat actually tells you something here.

There was probably a stew in this area already after 1380.

Madder — Rubia tinctorum — was important to the region for centuries. The roots of the plant were processed into a red powder. Woolen fabrics, among other things, could be dyed with this.

So red was not just a color here.

It was work.

Farmers grew madder. The roots were harvested and taken to the stew. There began an intensive process of drying, processing and grinding.

You can almost imagine the street with carts full of carrots, people lugging and the smell of a workshop.

Look at the old stepped gables at numbers 5 and 7. These seventeenth-century buildings give the street a special appearance.

The madder trade eventually disappeared as new dyes became increasingly important. However, the buildings remained standing.

[PAUSE — 2 sec]

This is how a historic street often works.

The work disappears.

The stones remain.

And if you know what you're looking at, those stones can tell the old story again.
