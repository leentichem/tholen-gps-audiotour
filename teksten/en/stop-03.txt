STOP 03 — BRIDGE STREET

[VOICE — personal]
Imagine this is your street.

You live here. You know the neighbors. You know which door always opens early, where someone is working and where children play in the street.

The Brugstraat is one of those streets where you see history not only in monuments, but especially in the scale of the houses.

For example, look at number 16. A large eighteenth-century mansion, with a bluestone sidewalk and a striking front door. The building has had different lives over the past centuries: distribution office, association building, school and ultimately a home again.

A little further on you see other reminders of everyday life. Number 3 was once a coach house. Number 23 has a much older core.

But try not to just look at the big houses.

Pay attention to front doors. On sidewalks. On windows. On the distance between the houses.

Behind every facade there was once a household with its own rhythm. People went to work, argued, had children, got sick, celebrated and made plans for tomorrow.

That makes an old street interesting.

Not only because the stones are old, but because people have lived here all this time.

[PAUSE — 2 sec]

Just look at the street as if you were coming back here tomorrow.

[TRANSITION]
Continue walking until you reach Hoogstraat. The town hall is there.
